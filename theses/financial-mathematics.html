Numerical Methods for Option Pricing with MATLAB.

Option Pricing using the Monte Carlo and Finite Difference Methods

INSTITUTIONTRINITY COLLEGE
YEAR2020
SUPERVISORProf. Ryan Pellico
TOPICSMonte Carlo, Black-Scholes

Abstract

We explore MATLAB programs for pricing and analyzing financial options. The first part covers Monte Carlo simulation methods, including European, lookback, Asian, binary, barrier, double-trigger, and range options, as well as spread options on correlated assets. It also covers American and Bermudan options priced with the Longstaff-Schwartz regression approach, and a simulation of the Heston stochastic volatility model. The second part covers numerical solutions to the Black-Scholes equation, using both an explicit finite difference scheme and a heat equation transformation, for European and American options. It also includes tools for computing the Greeks and implied volatility. Finally, the document presents a sensitivity and convergence analysis of the finite difference method, examining how grid size, time steps, maximum asset price, initial asset price, interest rate, and volatility affect the computed option values. Each program is described by its inputs, method, and outputs, and is illustrated with figures.
